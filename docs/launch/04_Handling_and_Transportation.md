# 04 — Dog handling and transportation policy

**DRAFT — Jordan and care lead must approve; insurance scope must match.**

## Proposed pilot standard

- One client household and one dog per walk initially. Additional household dogs require separate assessment, written scope and insurer confirmation; no mixed-client pack walks.
- Fixed-length leash and properly fitted approved collar/harness; check equipment and gates before handoff. Never knowingly use damaged or unmanageable equipment. No off-leash walks, dog parks, tying/unattended tethering or unsupervised interactions with unfamiliar animals.
- Owner supplies accurate behavior/health constraints; assign only a competent vetted walker. Defer dogs whose needs exceed available experience or coverage. No punishment-based handling, forced greetings or unsupported treatment/training promises.
- Water/shade and safe routes; check current heat, pavement, storms and alerts before each visit. Do not use a single numeric temperature as a guarantee of safety. Shorten/change to an agreed safe drop-in only with prior client consent; otherwise cancel and apply the approved refund policy.
- No swimming or shoreline access in the pilot. No boarding at Jordan's or a walker's home. No medication administration, veterinary diagnosis or overnight sitting until separately reviewed.
- Log arrival, handoff, agreed service, water/care observations, completion and issues. Photo updates exclude home security details, keys, exact location metadata and unrelated people.

## Transportation decision

**Proposed: no routine pet pickup/drop-off or vehicle transport.** Walks start/end at the approved customer handoff location. The native managed-service copy references pickup/return and recruiting asks about transportation; distinguish a walker's travel to work from transporting a client's dog. Reconcile that wording after approval.

Before any transport is offered, obtain written owner permission, driver/license and vehicle/restraint assessment, appropriate business-auto review and explicit pet-in-transit coverage. Do not assume ordinary personal auto insurance or CCC covers every driving risk. Never leave a pet unattended in a vehicle or transport loose/unrestrained animals.

For an emergency, contact the owner/authorized alternate and approved veterinary provider promptly. Follow the reviewed emergency consent, safe transport plan and spending authority. Use an authorized suitable driver or emergency transport service; do not promise that every walker can transport. Document attempted contacts. The exact urgency/authorization procedure requires professional and insurer review before launch.

## Incident and access SOP

1. Stop service and secure the dog/person if safe. Contact emergency services for immediate human danger and the appropriate veterinarian for animal emergencies.
2. Notify the designated manager and owner/alternate; give observed facts rather than diagnosis or blame. Record time, location privately, witnesses, contact attempts and actions.
3. For escape, initiate the approved lost-dog protocol with owner/manager; do not publish a customer address or identifying details without permission. For a bite, seek appropriate care and verify local reporting duties.
4. Preserve evidence privately and follow the insurer's reporting instructions. No public incident narrative or liability admissions from agents.
5. If entry is unavailable, do not force entry or leave the dog outside. Contact owner/manager, wait only the approved amount, record attempted access and apply reviewed cancellation terms.
6. On completion, verify secure return, gate/door closure and key custody; send a factual update. Escalate anything unresolved before dispatching the next visit.
