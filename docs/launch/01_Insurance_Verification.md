**Status: unconfirmed. Owner action required to obtain and bind coverage.**

## Complete the work

1. Confirm the legal operator/name to insure and the actual services, addresses/territory and staffing arrangement. Do not invent an LLC or list a trade name alone without insurer confirmation.
2. Obtain written quotations and policy wording from two specialist providers. Research starting points, not endorsements or bound quotes: [Pet Care Insurance](https://www.petcareins.com/dog-walkers-insurance) and [Pet Sitters Associates](https://www.petsitllc.com/pet-business-insurance-options).
3. Ask the insurer to identify the actual coverage/endorsement for animals in **care, custody or control (CCC / animal bailee)**. Ask separately about third-party bodily injury/property damage, vet expense without fault, lost keys, customer property and theft/bonding. These are different protections; a general-liability headline is insufficient.
4. Describe managers assigning local walkers, backups/substitutes and any independent contractors. Obtain written confirmation of who is covered, how each walker is added, and whether separate policies/additional insureds are needed. A walker's own policy does not establish the company's coverage.
5. Ask about travel between homes in personal vehicles even if dogs are not transported. If pet transport is contemplated, ask about animal injuries during transit and business auto/hired/non-owned liability. Do not assume pet-care coverage pays for vehicle damage or auto liability.
6. Compare limits, deductibles, exclusions, territorial limits, incident reporting and emergency authorization. Ask about bites, escape, pre-existing conditions, unattended care, multi-dog walks and home sitting. Keep out-of-scope services disabled.
7. Jordan selects/binds coverage only after reviewing cost and scope. Store policy, endorsements, effective dates and carrier confirmation privately; set a renewal reminder using the actual expiration date. No insurance purchase or insurer contact was made in preparing this pack.

## Staffing prerequisite

Before signing contractor agreements or dispatching workers, document the actual scheduling/control/payment arrangement and obtain qualified classification review. Texas Workforce Commission explains that the real direction/control relationship matters; calling someone a contractor does not settle it. No legal classification conclusion is made here. [TWC guidance](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/ics_contract_labor.html).
